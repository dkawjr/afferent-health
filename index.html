Positioning

Afferent is a clinical-AI firm built around a physician-scientist bench. We build models that read the entire high-resolution physiologic record - arterial pressure, capnography, pulse oximetry, EEG, laboratory values, and the electronic record - and turn it into early warning of costly events: death, postoperative delirium, unplanned ICU transfer, kidney injury, respiratory failure, and the chronic pain and long-term opioid use that begin in the same window. We choose the architecture that performs best. Not every such model can be made fully explainable, and we do not pretend otherwise; what we guarantee is that it is accountable. Where a model admits explanation, we can name the clinical and physiologic features it is using. Where it does not, we say so and earn trust by other means. We integrate practical clinical experience with AI modeling expertise to deliver products that are clinically implementable as well as scientifically sound.

What sets the work apart

We make powerful models deployable
On high-dimensional data the best-performing model is often not the most interpretable. Refusing it costs accuracy; fielding it blind fails the transparency and traceability principles of Executive Order 13960 that federal AI use requires. We do neither: our clinical bench verifies that predictions are physiologically coherent and leads the explainability analysis that makes a high-capacity model defensible.
We measure the problem before selling the solution
The VA surgical population is 60.0% frail or very frail against 21.3% in the private-sector NSQIP cohort (JAMA Surgery, 2022), the registry the standard surgical risk calculators are built from. Our first deliverable on any new population is a calibration audit, not a model.
The whole physiologic record
We use the full multimodal signal, not a convenient subset, because the earliest warning usually lives in the waveform, not the summary number.
Physician-scientist expertise to translate models into practice
Clinical judgment is in the loop from label definition through validation, the piece most engineering partners lack. It is the difference between a model that validates and a model that gets used.
Peer-reviewed science plus proprietary methods
Published clinical and computational work underwrites our methods. Our team has built both proprietary and openly published machine-learning methods for physiologic data, and is practiced at explaining them to regulators and reviewers in the terms those audiences require.
Public-private delivery, built to a measurable return
We operate comfortably as a commercial partner inside a government program and build to a case the sponsor can measure: the complications we predict are among the costliest in medicine, so earlier warning avoids spend as well as harm.
Evidence before assertion
Every claim is validated against controls and, where possible, external data before we make it, and we say plainly what has and has not been proven.

How we work

Partnership

We partner as the clinical bench

We work as a clinical subject-matter expert and subcontractor to integrators and health systems, supplying the validated models, medical judgment, and physician bench they need but do not house.

Design

Accountable, not merely simple

We pick the architecture that performs best, then do the work that makes it trustworthy: checking predictions against physiology and testing how far a model distills toward interpretable features before accuracy degrades. Where that limit is reached, we disclose it rather than dress it up.

Evidence

Validate before we assert

We test against controls, disclose our limitations, and hold our applied work to the standard of the peer-reviewed literature.

Custody

The model goes to the data

We design to bring the model to the data, respecting institutional custody and patient privacy rather than moving protected records off-site.

Direction

We are extending the multimodal approach across the perioperative and critical-care events where earlier, better-explained warning changes management: kidney injury, delirium, respiratory failure, hemodynamic instability, and persistent postoperative pain and long-term opioid use. Near-term work moves our internally validated results into external validation and the peer-reviewed record. As that evidence base matures, we bring these models to partners as validated, deployable clinical science.
